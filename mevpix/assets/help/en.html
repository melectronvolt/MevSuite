Measure lengths · version {{version}}

1. What is MeVPix for?

MeVPix measures lengths on an image: a photo taken with a phone, a microscope image, a map… It works in two stages:

  1. you show the program an object in the image whose length you know: the reference (for example 2 cm of graph paper);
  2. you draw segments on the image: MeVPix gives their real length.

In practical work, it is used for instance to measure the central spot of a diffraction pattern, the fringe spacing of an interference pattern, the size of a cell under the microscope or a distance on a map.

MeVPix also draws the intensity profile: the curve of the brightness along a line. The dark and bright fringes of an optics pattern are located there far more precisely than by eye.

2. The window

  1. 1Toolbar: open an image, choose the tool (📏 Calibrate, 📐 Measure, 📈 Intensity profile), see the whole image again (🔍 Fit to window); then the scale of the image.
  2. 2Theme and language: the sun (light theme) or the moon (dark theme), and the flag of the language. A click switches to the other theme or the other language.
  3. 3Tool panel: what to do, then the results (scale, table of measurements, profile cursors).
  4. 4The image, with the segments of the chosen tool: the reference in yellow (Calibrate), the measurements in blue (Measure) or the profile line in pink (Intensity profile).
  5. 5Status bar: on the left, what to do next or what just happened (in green, a success; in red, a problem); on the right, the open image and its zoom.

Lost? Read the status bar at the bottom of the window: it always tells you the next step.

3. Measuring in three steps

Step 1: open the image

On start-up, MeVPix shows its welcome page. Click 📂 Open an image…, in the middle of the window or in the toolbar (or Ctrl+O), or drag the image file onto the window. MeVPix reads PNG, JPEG, BMP, TIFF and WebP images.

iPhone photo (HEIC): MeVPix cannot read it. Email the photo to yourself (it arrives as JPEG), or set the camera to “Formats → Most Compatible”.

Step 2: calibrate

Calibration tells MeVPix how long one pixel of the image is. It is done for each image.

  1. The 📏 Calibrate tool is selected as soon as an image is open.
  2. Draw a segment, left-click and drag, from one end to the other of an object whose length you know: lines of the graph paper, marks of a ruler, scale bar of a microscope image. Holding Shift keeps the segment horizontal or vertical.
  3. Type its real length (for example “20”) and choose the unit (µm, mm, cm or m).
  4. Click ✔ Confirm calibration, or press Enter. The scale appears in the toolbar (“Scale: 1 px = 0.0206 mm”) and MeVPix switches to the Measure tool.

Step 3: measure

With the 📐 Measure tool, every segment you draw (left-click and drag) becomes a measurement. Its length appears on the image (M1, M2…) and in the table of the panel (see the figure in section 2).

  • Correct a measurement: grab an end point (round handle) and move it.
  • Delete a measurement: the bin 🗑 next to it, or Ctrl+Z for the last one; Clear all deletes them all.
  • Keep the results: 📋 Copy the table, then Ctrl+V in a spreadsheet, Regressi or your report.

4. Measuring well

Pointing precisely

  • Zoom with the mouse wheel (the zoom is centred on the mouse), then place each end point to the nearest pixel.
  • Move the image with the right mouse button held down.
  • 🔍 Fit to window shows the whole image again.

Choosing a good reference

  • Long: a 1-pixel error at each end matters much less on a long reference. Over 50 px (1 mm of graph paper), the error is 4 % on every measurement; over 1000 px (2 cm), 0.2 %.
  • In the same plane as the measured object, with the camera facing it squarely: otherwise perspective distorts the lengths.

Precision of the result

MeVPix shows three significant figures (“20.2 mm”): this is rounding, not an uncertainty. The uncertainty mostly comes from pointing, about 1 pixel at each end. With the scale “1 px = 0.0206 mm”, that is about 0.04 mm on a measurement. Pointing the same end points several times shows the spread of your measurements.

Reference changed? If you move the reference or change its length or unit, a ⚠ warning asks you to confirm again. After confirming, the measurements already drawn are recalculated: nothing is lost.

Without calibration, lengths are in pixels (“px”) and the warning “⚠ Not calibrated” stays visible.

5. The intensity profile

The 📈 Intensity profile tool draws the curve of the brightness along a profile line. On a diffraction or interference pattern, the dips of the curve are the dark fringes.

  1. Choose the tool: the graph appears below the image.
  2. Draw the profile line on the image, along the pattern (left-click and drag; Shift for a horizontal line).
  3. Place the cursors A and B: drag them on the image (they stay on the profile line), or click and drag on the graph.
  4. Read their positions and the gap Δx = |xB − xA| on the left.

Channel and thickness

  • Channel: luminance (grey) combines the three colours; the Red channel often brings out the fringes of a red laser better.
  • Thickness: the intensity is averaged over a band on both sides of the profile line. 10 to 20 px smooth out the grid of the paper and the noise of the photo.

Saturation

“⚠ … % of the line is saturated”: the photo reaches the maximum value 255 there, and the real intensity is higher than shown. This is common in the centre of a diffraction pattern. The positions of the minima remain correct; the heights of saturated maxima do not.

The graph

  • Mouse wheel: zoom; Shift+wheel: move; double-click: see the whole curve again.
  • Hovering over the curve shows the position and intensity of the point.

Exporting the profile

💾 Export the profile (CSV)… saves the curve in a file with two columns: the position x and the intensity I (from 0 to 255). It opens in LibreOffice Calc, Excel or Regressi. In Python:

import pandas as pd
profile = pd.read_csv("profile.csv")
profile.plot(x="x (mm)", y="I")

6. Example: diffraction by a slit

A laser of wavelength λ lights a slit of width a. On a screen at distance D, a central spot of width L appears, surrounded by smaller spots. When D is large compared with L:

L = 2 λ D / a so a = 2 λ D / L

  1. Photograph the pattern on graph paper fixed to the screen, with the camera facing it squarely. Note λ (written on the laser) and measure D.
  2. Open the photo in MeVPix.
  3. Calibrate on the thick lines of the graph paper (one every centimetre): for example 2 cm, i.e. “20” and the unit mm.
  4. Measure L from one dark minimum to the other around the central spot, with the Measure tool, or better with the intensity profile (cursors A and B on the two minima: L = Δx).
  5. Calculate a. With L = 20.2 mm, λ = 650 nm and D = 2.00 m: a = 2 × 650 × 10−9 × 2.00 / 0.0202 ≈ 1.29 × 10−4 m, i.e. about 129 µm.

Measure wider to measure better. The two third-order minima are 3 L apart. Placing A and B on these minima and dividing Δx by 3 also divides the pointing error by 3.

Young's slits: the interference fringes are spaced by i = λ D / b (b: distance between the slits). Measure the distance across N + 1 fringes and divide by N to get i.

8. Troubleshooting

The “Confirm calibration” button stays greyed out.
The segment is missing (draw it on the image), or the length is not a positive number. Type only the number (“20”, not “20 mm”): the unit is chosen in the list next to it.
Lengths are shown in “px”.
The image is not calibrated: do step 2. The measurements already drawn will be converted.
“Unsupported image format”.
The file is not a PNG, JPEG, BMP, TIFF or WebP image. An iPhone photo (HEIC) or a PDF must first be converted to JPEG.
The mouse wheel does not zoom.
The mouse must be over the image, not over the panel or the graph.
The text has become huge (or tiny).
Ctrl+0 restores the normal size.
Ctrl+Z does nothing.
It deletes the last measurement only with the Measure tool, and when you are not typing in a field.
The profile graph is empty.
Draw a profile line on the image with the Intensity profile tool.
My measurements have disappeared.
Opening another image clears the calibration and the measurements (MeVPix asks first). Copy the table of measurements before changing image.
The program closed by itself.
An unexpected error occurred. Start MeVPix again and tell your teacher.

9. Installing and using in class

  • Windows, no installation: MeVPix fits in a folder (.zip archive), to copy onto the computer, the school network or a USB stick; run mevpix.exe. It works offline, guide included. The first time it runs on a computer, Windows may show “Windows protected your PC”, because MeVPix is not digitally signed: click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. To open an image directly: right-click on the image → “Open with…” → MeVPix.
  • Windows, installer: MeVPix-version-windows-x64-setup.exe installs MeVPix for all users in Program Files\MeVSuite, with a Start menu shortcut; it is uninstalled from the Windows Settings. Deployment on many computers (WAPT, GPO): options /VERYSILENT /SUPPRESSMSGBOXES /NORESTART.
  • Linux: .deb package (Ubuntu, Debian), .rpm (Fedora), or .AppImage, which runs without installation (make it executable).
  • Large photos: a phone photo (12 to 50 Mpx) opens in a few seconds (“Loading…” in the status bar). The display is reduced if needed, but measurements and the profile always use the original pixels.
  • Projector: Ctrl++ enlarges the text and the buttons (the enlargement is capped so that the panels stay visible); Ctrl+0 returns to the normal size.

10. Settings

On first launch, MeVPix uses the language and the light or dark theme of the system (English if the language is neither French nor English). The sun/moon and flag buttons change the theme and the language; numbers follow the language (decimal point in English).

MeVPix remembers from one session to the next: the language, the theme, the unit of the reference, the channel and thickness of the profile, the text enlargement, the last folder opened, the size of the window. These settings are in a small text file mevpix.ini, specific to each user and editable by hand; deleting it restores the defaults.

SystemLocation
Windows%APPDATA%\MeVSuite\mevpix.ini
Linux~/.config/MeVSuite/mevpix.ini

Enforced settings (computer room, USB stick): a mevpix.ini file placed next to mevpix.exe is read at every launch and takes priority over the user's settings; MeVPix never writes it. For example:

language = fr
theme = light

enforces French and the light theme on every computer. The possible keys are those of the user's file; language and theme also accept auto (language or theme of the system). The paths of both files are shown in Help → About MeVPix.

The four programs of the suite (MeVPix, MeVCine, MeVReg, MeVSono) keep their settings in this same MeVSuite folder. The guide is written each time it is opened to the system's temporary folder (mevpix-help): it can be printed or saved from the browser.

11. Errors, contact and licence

Unexpected error: MeVPix shows a message before closing and saves the details in mevpix.log, in the same folder as mevpix.ini. Attach this file to your report.
