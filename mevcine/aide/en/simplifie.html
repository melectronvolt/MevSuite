Simple mode, step by step

From the first frame of interest to a table of positions in metres

On the left, at the top, the magnifier and the coordinates of the point under the cursor; below, the instruction of the step; at the very bottom, Previous and Next. In the middle, the video frame, and under it the choice of the frame. On the right, from step 4 on, the table of measurements. The whole window is described on the Overview page.

The top bar highlights the current step. The Next button becomes available once you complete the action. You can revisit steps 1 to 3 after marking. If you change the axes or scale, MeVCine keeps your marks and recalculates their coordinates.

Step 1: the origin of the axes

Click on the frame where you want the origin O: often the starting position of the object, or a point easy to find (the end of a ruler, the ground…). The origin appears in green. To move it, click elsewhere, or drag it: with the button held down, the mouse carries it along.

Is the video lying on its side? The To the left and To the right buttons rotate it by a quarter turn (only before the scale and the marking).

Use this moment to browse the video with the slider under the frame: find where the motion starts and where it ends.

Step 2: the direction of the axes

Choose the directions of x and y by clicking the matching pair of axes. For a throw, try pointing y upwards. The value of y should increase as the ball rises. For a drop, pointing y downwards gives a positive vertical velocity during the fall.

You can still drag the green origin. Moving it changes the position coordinates. Reversing an axis changes the sign of the corresponding velocity component.

Step 3: the scale

  1. Click on Select the reference. On the frame, click on one end of an object of known length (a ruler, a door, a measured distance), then on the other end. You can also drag from one end to the other. Right-click or Esc: cancel.
  2. Type its real length, choose the unit in the list (mm, cm or m), then Confirm (or Enter). You can also write the unit after the number: 85 cm.

The message “Scale: 85 cm for 353 pixels.” confirms the scale. The reference stays drawn on the frame with its length. Positions are then given in metres, whatever the unit of the reference. How to choose a good reference, and all the allowed ways to write it: The scale.

Step 4: marking

  1. With the slider (or the ← → arrows), go to the first useful frame: the one where the motion starts. That will be time t = 0.
  2. Click on the object. The next frame shows up by itself, and the table gains a row. Do it again, frame after frame.
  3. Look at the magnifier, top left, when you click, and always aim at the same point of the object: its centre, or its front edge.
  • Undo the last point (Ctrl+Z) erases the last point and goes back to its frame; ↷ (Ctrl+Y) brings it back.
  • No need to mark the whole video: stop when the motion is over. However, you cannot skip a frame.
  • Slow-motion video? Open this section if the video was filmed in slow motion, and enter the capture frame rate (for instance 240 frames per second). See In slow motion.
  • “Variable frame rate: the real times of the frames are used.”: the video comes from a phone whose frames are not evenly spaced. Nothing to do: MeVCine takes the real time of each frame. See Variable frame rate.

When your measurements are ready, click Finish marking to open 5. Export.

Checking and correcting measurements while marking

In the table to the right of the video, find the row by its time t in seconds. Read x and y in the units shown in the header. Hover over the row to see the frame number in the preview. At a constant 25 frames/s, successive frames are 0.040 s apart.

  • Hover over a coordinate to circle its object. The other marks stay visible. The arrow keys also move the preview between cells.
  • To mark a frame again, click the target ⌖ in Redo, then mark the object in the displayed frame. Esc cancels the correction.
  • Click the bin in Del. to remove the whole row. To remove one object, select its coordinate and press Delete. Ctrl+Z undoes the deletion.

Marking faster with assisted marking

  1. In step 4, turn on Assisted marking. Click a feature you can follow throughout the motion, such as the centre of the ball. This first click provides a reference for that object.
  2. Look at the magnifier in the next frame. If the crosshair is on the feature, click the centre or press Space. This records the measurement and moves to the next frame or object.
  3. If the feature is off-centre, click its actual position in the magnifier or the video. This adds an example of how it looks in that frame. If the object rotates, keep marking the same physical feature.

MeVCine checks your examples near the last measured position and can place the crosshair between pixels. If it cannot recognise the feature or the examples point to different places, “Feature unclear” asks you to mark it in the video. If an example was misplaced, use Redo in its row to correct it.

Hold Shift and press an arrow to move the crosshair by one video pixel. Press Space to keep this correction as a reference. Confirming an untouched crosshair records only the measurement. Each object’s references are available when you reopen the project.

Marking in full screen

Click Full screen below the video or press F11 to enlarge it. With assisted marking, the magnifier stays to the left of the video. Click in the magnifier or on the object in the video as you would in the window. When marking freely, the panel changes sides as the pointer approaches to keep the frame clear.

  • At the bottom: the choice of the frame, ↶ ↷ to undo or redo the last point.
  • Leave full screen (Esc or F11) goes back to the window; marking goes on.
  • Finish marking leaves full screen and opens 5. Export.

Step 5 · Exporting the measurements

Choose Finish marking in step 4. Check the table on the right. Choose Previous to return to marking if a measurement needs correcting.

  • Save the project, to continue later… keeps the scale, axes, frame rate and measurements. Take the original video with you as well.
  • Copy for Python supplies NumPy arrays. For one object their names are lst_t, lst_x and lst_y.
  • Save as CSV… creates columns for a spreadsheet. Read their units in the header row.

A session saved during marking resumes at step 4 once the axes and scale are ready. A completed automatic run saved in expert mode opens at Export in the guide when those steps are complete. See the export examples.
