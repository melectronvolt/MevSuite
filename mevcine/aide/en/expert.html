Expert mode

All the features, in four tabs

Expert mode gives access to everything: several objects, automatic tracking, trajectories, velocity vectors, chronophotography, change of reference frame, grapher. You switch to it with the Mode › Expert mode (all features) menu; the measurements made in simple mode are kept, and marking goes on. The four tabs, in the order of the work: Marking, Coordinates, Trajectories, Grapher. The last three open once marking has started.

Marking tab

At the top: the axes, in three steps

1. Origin — Place the origin
Then click on the frame where you want it (pressing the button again, or Esc, gives up). Before marking, the origin can also be dragged. Possible even after marking: the measurements are recomputed.
2. Axes — four small sets of axes
A click chooses the direction of x and y; the chosen one is outlined. With the mouse on a button, its direction is given in words (“x to the right, y upward”).
3. Scale — Set the scale
Draw the reference on the frame (two clicks, or a drag), then give its length and unit (mm, cm or m) in the small window. Next to the button: “85 cm for 353 px”, or “not set: the measurements are in pixels”. The button then becomes Redo the scale. See The scale.

While choosing the origin or the reference, the instruction shows below these buttons, in orange, with Cancel.

On the left: the magnifier, the objects, the video

Magnifier and coordinates
The frame enlarged around the mouse, and the coordinates of the point under the cursor, in px and in m.
Objects to track
The number of objects (1 to 10), before starting. With several objects, you mark object 1, then object 2… on each frame: the magnifier's title says which one is expected, and each object has its own colour.
Automatic tracking
Tick it before starting so that MeVCine follows the objects by itself (below).
Start marking
Each click on the object records its position and shows the next frame. First go to the first useful frame. No gaps are allowed: to resume elsewhere, undo the last points.
⏹ Stop marking
Marking is over: a click on the image no longer marks, and the Coordinates tab opens. The measurements are kept; ⌖ still redoes a measurement. ▶ Resume marking goes on.
Video
The name of the video, its size, its number of frames, its duration; and the Frames per second, detected in the video. For slow motion, enter the capture frame rate (for instance 240). “Variable frame rate”, in orange, means the frames are not evenly spaced (phone): their real times are used, and the Frames per second only give the usual frame rate of the camera. See Variable frame rate.
Start over
Erases the measurements, the scale and the axes; the video stays open. MeVCine asks for confirmation if there are measurements.

Under the frame: ⟲ ⟳ rotate the video by a quarter turn (before the scale and marking); the frame number and the slider; ↶ ↷ undo or redo the last point; Full screen (F11), while marking. The status bar, at the bottom, says what to do (“Click on the object”, “Click on object 2”…).

Marking in full screen

Full screen gives the whole screen to the image. The magnifier and the coordinates stay visible in a frame at the top of the image: it moves to the other side when the mouse comes near, so that it never hides the object. The instruction is under the magnifier; at the bottom, the choice of the frame, ✕ Leave full screen (Esc), which goes back to the window without stopping the marking, and ⏹ Stop marking. Full screen also exists in simple mode, at step 4: see Marking in full screen, with a picture.

Automatic tracking

  1. Enter the number of objects, tick Automatic tracking, go to the first useful frame, then Start marking.
  2. After the message, draw a box (drag) around each object, on the frame shown. The box should contain the object, well contrasted, with little background.
  3. MeVCine looks for each object from frame to frame, and places the points. The red STOP button stops it; it stops by itself at the last frame.

Then check the points: a misplaced point is redone with ⌖ (Coordinates tab). Tracking works well for a sharp object, with a clear colour, on a plain background; it gets lost if the object rotates, changes size or passes in front of a similar background.

Coordinates tab

The table takes the whole tab: the time t (zero at the first marked frame) and the positions of each object, in metres (x1, y1, x2… with several objects). The ⌖ button of a row redoes that measurement: the Marking tab opens on the frame, the point is circled; click on the object, and you come back to the table.

Above it, on one line, Export the measurements: 📋 Copy for Python (“✔ Copied” confirms it), 💾 Save as CSV…, Save the project…; the mouse resting on a button says what it does. See Exporting the measurements.

Trajectories tab

Trajectory
The points, the axes and, if you tick Show the velocity vectors, one arrow per point. Each vector is computed from the previous and next positions. Their length is chosen automatically; to set it, type the number of pixels for 1 m/s in Velocity scale (empty box: automatic; the tooltip of the box recalls it). The legend, bottom left, shows the arrow of a round speed. Near the mouse draws only the vector of the point under the mouse.
Chronophotography
The points on a frame of the video (Frame no. chooses which), with the interval Δt between two frames (“usual Δt” if the frame rate is variable), the time of the frame, and the reference in blue.
Motion diagram
The points alone, named M0, M1, M2…, with the reference, on the background of the graphs: white in the light theme, dark in the dark theme. To print it and work on paper, first switch to the light theme (the sun, at the top right).
Save
Saves the drawing as an image (PNG or JPEG).
Change of reference frame
Choose object no. 1 instead of camera: the points are drawn in the frame of reference where this object is still. Watch the video opens the video cropped so that the object stays still, with an adjustable slow motion.

Grapher tab

At the top, choose what to plot (Plot) and against what (versus, t by default): t, x, y, the speed |v| and its components vx, vy, the acceleration |a|, ax, ay. The graph, empty at first, is drawn at once, with its title and the labels of its axes. Three styles (Style): Points only, Points and lines, Lines only. Save writes the graph as PNG, title included.

The velocities and accelerations of the grapher are quick estimates, from differences between two successive frames, with the real time of each frame. They are for exploring the motion and are not exported: the proper calculation is yours to do, from the positions.

Saving, resuming

File › Save the project… (Ctrl+S) keeps everything; File › Open a project… resumes where you were. See Projects.
