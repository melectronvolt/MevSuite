Expert mode

All the features, in four tabs

  • Marking
  • Automatic tracking
  • Coordinates
  • Trajectories
  • Plotter
  • How derivatives are calculated

Expert mode gives access to everything: several objects, automatic tracking, trajectories, velocity vectors, chronophotography, change of reference frame, grapher. You switch to it with the Mode › Expert mode (all features) menu; the measurements made in simple mode are kept, and marking goes on. The four tabs, in the order of the work: Marking, Coordinates, Trajectories, Grapher. The last three open once marking has started.

Marking tab

At the top: the axes, in three steps

1. Origin — Place the origin
Then click on the frame where you want it (pressing the button again, or Esc, gives up). Before marking, the origin can also be dragged. Possible even after marking: the measurements are recomputed.
2. Axes — four small sets of axes
A click chooses the direction of x and y; the chosen one is outlined. With the mouse on a button, its direction is given in words (“x to the right, y upward”).
3. Scale — Set the scale
Draw the reference on the frame (two clicks, or a drag), then give its length and unit (mm, cm or m) in the small window. Next to the button: “85 cm for 353 px”, or “not set: the measurements are in pixels”. The button then becomes Redo the scale. See The scale.

While choosing the origin or the reference, the instruction shows below these buttons, in orange, with Cancel.

On the left: the magnifier, the objects, the video

Magnifier and coordinates
The frame enlarged around the mouse, and the coordinates of the point under the cursor, in px and in m.
Objects to track
The number of objects (1 to 10), before starting. With several objects, you mark object 1, then object 2… on each frame: the magnifier's title says which one is expected, and each object has its own colour.
Automatic tracking
Tick it before starting so that MeVCine follows the objects by itself (below).
Start marking
Each click on the object records its position and shows the next frame. First go to the first useful frame. No gaps are allowed: to resume elsewhere, undo the last points.
Stop marking
Marking is over: a click on the image no longer marks, and the Coordinates tab opens. The measurements are kept; ⌖ still redoes a measurement. Resume marking goes on.
Video
The name of the video, its size, its number of frames, its duration; and the Frames per second, detected in the video. For slow motion, enter the capture frame rate (for instance 240). “Variable frame rate”, in orange, means the frames are not evenly spaced (phone): their real times are used, and the Frames per second only give the usual frame rate of the camera. See Variable frame rate.
Start over
Erases the measurements, the scale and the axes; the video stays open. MeVCine asks for confirmation if there are measurements.

Under the frame: ⟲ ⟳ rotate the video by a quarter turn (before the scale and marking); the frame number and the slider; ↶ ↷ undo or redo the last point; Full screen (F11), while marking. The status bar, at the bottom, says what to do (“Click on the object”, “Click on object 2”…).

The small table to the right of the video lists the marked frame numbers. Hover over a number to circle the objects marked in that frame. Points from the other frames stay visible. Click the target ⌖ in Redo to mark the frame again or the bin to delete all its points. Press Ctrl+Z to restore them together.

Marking in full screen

Full screen gives the whole screen to the image. The magnifier and the coordinates stay visible in a frame at the top of the image: it moves to the other side when the mouse comes near, so that it never hides the object. The instruction is under the magnifier; at the bottom, the choice of the frame, Leave full screen (Esc), which goes back to the window without stopping the marking, and Stop marking. Full screen also exists in simple mode, at step 4: see Marking in full screen, with a picture.

Automatic tracking

  1. Enter the number of objects, tick Automatic tracking, go to the first useful frame, then Start marking.
  2. Draw a box (drag) around each object, on the frame shown. The box should contain the object, well contrasted, with little background.
  3. MeVCine tracks each object from frame to frame and marks its position. After the last frame, marking ends and the Coordinates tab opens. To switch to manual marking before the end, click STOP.

Then check the points: a misplaced point is redone with ⌖ (Coordinates tab). Tracking works well for a sharp object, with a clear colour, on a plain background; it gets lost if the object rotates, changes size or passes in front of a similar background.

MeVCine first searches around the position predicted from the last reliable frames and their timestamps. If the local search fails, it searches the rest of the frame. It compares the colour and details of the selected feature. When two objects overlap, review the flagged points before using those measurements. An uncertain position does not update the motion estimate for the next frame.

The robot marks automatic measurements. Next point to review opens the frame of an uncertain or lost object. Click the object to correct its position. Choose Accept point if the proposed position is correct. If the object has disappeared, Confirm absence keeps the position missing and completes the review. A dash marks a lost object in the table.

Coordinates tab

The table takes the whole tab: the time t (zero at the first marked frame) and the positions of each object, in metres after calibration or pixels before calibration (x1, y1, x2… with several objects). The ⌖ button of a row redoes that measurement: the Marking tab opens on the frame, the point is circled; click on the object, and you come back to the table.

Hover over a coordinate to circle its object in the preview, or over the time to circle all objects in the frame. The bin deletes the whole row. To remove one object, select its coordinate and press Delete.

Above it, on one line, Export the measurements: Copy for Python, Save as CSV…, Save the project…; the mouse resting on a button says what it does. See Exporting the measurements.

Trajectories tab

Trajectory
The points, the axes and, if you tick Show the velocity vectors, one arrow per point. Each vector uses the central position, its two neighbours and their times. Their length is chosen automatically; to set it, type the number of pixels for 1 m/s in Velocity scale (empty box: automatic; the tooltip of the box recalls it). The legend, bottom left, shows the arrow of a round speed. Near the mouse draws only the vector of the point under the mouse.
Chronophotography
The points on a frame of the video (Frame no. chooses which), with the interval Δt between two frames (“usual Δt” if the frame rate is variable), the time of the frame, and the reference in blue.
Motion diagram
The points alone, named M0, M1, M2…, with the reference, on the background of the graphs: white in the light theme, dark in the dark theme. To print it and work on paper, first switch to the light theme (the sun, at the top right).
Save
Saves the drawing as an image (PNG or JPEG).
Change of reference frame
Choose object no. 1 instead of camera: the points are drawn in the frame of reference where this object is still. Watch the video opens the video cropped so that the object stays still, with an adjustable slow motion.

In the first object’s frame of reference, the second object’s relative position is the difference between their positions in the same frame. Frames missing the reference object are omitted from the drawing. The cropped video preserves the elapsed time between the available frames. The table and exports keep the session coordinates in the camera frame.

Grapher tab

Choose a vertical quantity such as y in Plot and a horizontal quantity such as t in versus. The title, tick marks and units follow these choices. You can plot position, velocity and acceleration components and the magnitudes of velocity and acceleration.

  • Points only shows the values available for each frame. Hover over a cross to read both coordinates and its frame number below the graph.
  • Points and lines and Lines only connect consecutive frames with a smooth curve through every point. Move the pointer along the line. The crosshair jumps from one measurement to the next. Below the plot, read the coordinates of the circled point and its frame number.
  • A frame with a missing value leaves a gap. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan and double-click to show all. Save writes a PNG with the title and axes.

Where does a velocity or acceleration value come from?

Plot vy against t and click a point. The window shows three positions and their times. The result belongs to the middle frame. The same object must be marked in both neighbouring frames.

At a constant interval Δt, three consecutive vertical coordinates give vy = (y3 − y1) / (2 Δt) and ay = (y3 − 2 y2 + y1) / Δt². The same calculation applies to x. A small marking error is particularly noticeable in the acceleration.

At a variable frame rate, MeVCine fits a parabola through the three positions at their actual times. Its slope and second derivative at the middle time give velocity and acceleration. Endpoints and missing positions leave gaps in the graph.

Saving, resuming

Ctrl+S keeps the marks and their corrections. If marking had stopped when the project was saved, it opens on Coordinates. To add a point, open the Marking tab and choose Resume marking. A session interrupted between two objects resumes with the next object in that frame.

To move a session to another computer, take the film and the project together. To hand in data for analysis, use a CSV file.
