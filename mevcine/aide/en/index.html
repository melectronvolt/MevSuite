MeVCine

Measuring a filmed motion — the user guide, for students first, then for teachers

MeVCine is used to measure a filmed motion: a falling ball, a sliding puck, a passing cyclist… You open the video, choose a set of axes (an origin and the direction of the axes) and a scale (an object of known length), then click on the object, frame after frame. MeVCine then gives, for each frame, the time t and the coordinates x and y of the object. You copy them into a Python program, or save them to a file for a spreadsheet.

You do the physics. MeVCine measures times and positions, nothing else: it asks for neither the mass nor g. Velocities, accelerations and energies are your job, for instance in a Python program.

Six steps

  1. Open a video: your own (phone, camera), or your teacher's.
  2. Place the origin of the axes: a click on the frame, or a drag.
  3. Choose the direction of the axes x and y, among four drawn sets of axes.
  4. Set the scale: draw a reference on the frame, then type its length (in mm, cm or m).
  5. Mark the object frame after frame: the table of measurements fills in.
  6. Export: copy for Python, or save as CSV.

Simple mode goes through these steps one by one, with an instruction each time. MeVCine offers it on first launch: it is the one to choose to get started.

The window

  1. 1The menus: File (open a video, save the project…), Edit, Mode (simple or expert, at any time, without losing the measurements) and Help (this guide, F1).
  2. 2Theme and language: the sun (light theme) or the moon (dark theme), and the flag of the language. A click switches to the other theme, or to the other language.
  3. 3The six steps of simple mode. A tick ✔ marks the steps done; a click on a step goes back to it.
  4. 4The magnifier enlarges the frame around the mouse. Next to it, the coordinates of the point under the cursor, in your axes: in pixels (px), then in metres (m) once the scale is set.
  5. 5The instruction of the step: what to do, and its buttons.
  6. 6◀ Previous and Next ▶: change step. They are always visible.
  7. 7The video frame, with the axes (in green), the reference and its length, and your points (red crosses).
  8. 8Choosing the frame: its number, the slider, and the ↶ ↷ buttons to undo or redo the last point. Full screen: to mark, the image fills the whole screen, the magnifier stays visible (Esc to come back).
  9. 9The table of measurements: t, x, y for each marked frame, and the ⌖ button to redo a measurement.
  10. 10The status bar: on the left, what to do next, or what just happened (green for a success, orange for an expected action, red for a problem); on the right, the open video (its size, its frames per second), the frame shown and the mode.

Lost? Read the status bar, at the very bottom: it always tells you what to do next. And the F1 key opens the page of this guide that matches what you are doing.

Two ways of working

Simple modeThe six steps, one by one, for a single object. To get started, and for most labs.
Expert modeAll the features, in four tabs: Marking, Coordinates, Trajectories, Grapher. Several objects, automatic tracking, velocity vectors, chronophotography, change of reference frame, graphs.

The Mode menu changes mode at any time, without losing the measurements. Simple mode follows only one object: if your measurements have several, MeVCine offers to keep only object 1. MeVCine starts in the mode used last time (on first launch, it asks for it); the status bar recalls it, bottom right.
